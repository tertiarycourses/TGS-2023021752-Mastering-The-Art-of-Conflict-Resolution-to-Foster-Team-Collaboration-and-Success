# Activity 03 — Conflict Style Choice Clinic at BrightWorks Digital

**Course:** Mastering The Art of Conflict Resolution to Foster Team Collaboration and Success (TGS-2023021752)  
**Duration:** 45 minutes  
**Materials:** Style decision matrix  
**Output:** A conflict-style decision matrix and staged response for the BrightWorks security-versus-launch dispute.

## Objective

Select and justify a conflict style based on stakes, urgency, relationship and power.

## Psychological-safety boundary

Use only the fictional case. Do not require anyone to disclose a personal conflict. Any participant may call a pause or request another role. This learning activity does not replace organisational policy, HR advice or legal advice.

## Scenario

BrightWorks Digital's product and sales teams disagree about releasing a feature before security testing is complete. Sales promises a launch date to a key client; Product says the unresolved issue may expose customer data.
The CEO wants a decision today, but both teams must work together for the next six months. Learners assess five possible styles and explain a staged response rather than declaring one style universally best.

## Detailed instructions

1. **Frame the issue.** State the decision in one neutral sentence and list the shared interests of client trust, product value, security and continued collaboration.

2. **Rate five conditions.** For safety, outcome importance, relationship importance, power/voice and time pressure, choose low, medium or high and cite case evidence.

3. **Review each style.** For compete, collaborate, compromise, avoid and accommodate, write one potential benefit and one risk in this case.

4. **Identify non-negotiables.** Name any legal, ethical, safety, security or policy threshold that cannot be traded for speed or harmony.

5. **Choose the immediate style.** Select the approach for today's deadline. Explain who has authority and how dissenting expertise will still be heard.

6. **Choose the follow-on style.** Select a different or continuing approach for the next two weeks so the immediate decision does not become the entire resolution.

7. **Protect voice.** Add a mechanism for technical and commercial concerns to be raised without retaliation or domination by title.

8. **Draft the staged response.** Write the action, owner, timing, communication and review point for stage one and stage two.

9. **Stress-test the choice.** Consider what happens if the client refuses a delay, if the risk is less severe than thought, or if the CEO insists on release.

10. **Gallery review.** Compare decisions with another group and identify where a preferred personal style may have replaced situational judgment.

## Suggested timing

- 5 minutes — Issue and shared interests
- 8 minutes — Condition ratings
- 8 minutes — Style benefits and risks
- 7 minutes — Non-negotiables and authority
- 7 minutes — Staged response
- 5 minutes — Stress test
- 5 minutes — Gallery review

## Discussion questions

- Which part of the issue is non-negotiable, and who has authority to decide it?
- Where would collaboration add value after the immediate decision?
- What power imbalance or voice risk must the facilitator manage?
- What temporary agreement could protect the client relationship without compromising security?

## Submission

Submit the completed worksheet and checklist. Use role names rather than real personal data, mark unresolved information as unknown and retain the artefact only through the approved course channel.

## Debrief

The recommendation links each style choice to evidence about safety, stakes, relationship, power and time.
