# Activity 08 — 30-Day Resolution Review at GreenGrid Engineering

**Course:** Mastering The Art of Conflict Resolution to Foster Team Collaboration and Success (TGS-2023021752)  
**Duration:** 55 minutes  
**Materials:** Review dashboard · prevention plan  
**Output:** A 30-day resolution review dashboard, learning review and recurrence-prevention plan with owners and escalation triggers.

## Objective

Evaluate resolution effectiveness and design controls that prevent recurrence.

## Psychological-safety boundary

Use only the fictional case. Do not require anyone to disclose a personal conflict. Any participant may call a pause or request another role. This learning activity does not replace organisational policy, HR advice or legal advice.

## Scenario

GreenGrid Engineering mediated a conflict between design lead Noor and site lead Ethan after repeated late drawing changes caused rework. They agreed on respectful language, a 3:00pm change cut-off and a weekly coordination meeting.
Thirty days later, meeting tone has improved and both report less stress, but three changes still arrived after the cut-off. Two were safety-related; one resulted from unclear approval authority. Learners evaluate effectiveness and design recurrence controls.

## Detailed instructions

## Role cards

Give each participant only their own role before the first round. Observers use the checklist.

### Review Lead

**Goal:** evaluate fairly and improve the system.  
**Private brief:** Uses evidence, protects confidentiality and separates outcomes from blame.

### Noor · Design Lead

**Goal:** protect design integrity.  
**Private brief:** Reports better dialogue but unclear authority for urgent changes.

### Ethan · Site Lead

**Goal:** reduce rework and schedule risk.  
**Private brief:** Sees improvement but wants a faster exception route.

1. **Restate the agreement.** Summarise the GreenGrid agreement as observable commitments, owners, dates, intended outcomes and review measures.

2. **Select balanced indicators.** Choose leading and lagging measures across behaviour, process, work results, people experience and risk. Avoid using harmony or silence as the only evidence.

3. **Define baselines and targets.** For each measure, record the starting point, 30-day target, data source, collection frequency and owner.

4. **Review evidence.** Use the mock data to compare baseline, week two and day 30. Mark positive, mixed or negative movement and confidence in the data.

5. **Seek affected voices.** Plan brief confidential check-ins that ask about fairness, safety, workload, trust and whether commitments are occurring in practice.

6. **Check unintended effects.** Look for avoidance, hidden retaliation, workarounds, unequal burden, superficial compliance or a transfer of the problem elsewhere.

7. **Decide effectiveness.** Classify the resolution as effective, partially effective or ineffective and write an evidence-based rationale.

8. **Run a learning review.** Identify what helped, what failed, what assumption was wrong and what organisational condition allowed recurrence.

9. **Design prevention controls.** Choose controls at individual, team, workflow, policy and leadership levels. Prioritise fixes to decision rights, handoffs, incentives and feedback channels.

10. **Assign owners and triggers.** Give every action an owner and date, and define the evidence or event that triggers escalation or a new intervention.

11. **Communicate closure carefully.** Draft a closure update that confirms process and commitments without exposing confidential details or declaring relationships repaired without evidence.

12. **Present the dashboard.** Deliver a two-minute review to a mock leadership panel and answer one challenge about measurement quality or recurrence risk.

## Suggested timing

- 6 minutes — Agreement and measures
- 8 minutes — Baselines, targets and sources
- 10 minutes — Mock-data review
- 7 minutes — Voice and unintended-effect check
- 7 minutes — Effectiveness decision
- 8 minutes — Prevention plan
- 4 minutes — Leadership briefing

## Discussion questions

- Which parts of the original resolution were effective, partly effective or ineffective?
- What evidence is needed beyond self-report?
- Which recurrence cause is behavioural, and which is structural?
- What preventive control, owner, date and trigger should be added?

## Submission

Submit the completed worksheet and checklist. Use role names rather than real personal data, mark unresolved information as unknown and retain the artefact only through the approved course channel.

## Debrief

The review reaches an evidence-based verdict and adds preventive controls with ownership, timing and measurable follow-up.
